CLASS 01 · Domain Spine: Dependability

Topics: 5 Attributes (Availability, Reliability, Safety, Security, Resilience), Sources of Failure

My Evidence (V1):

IntelliTA – Dependability Mapping

I mapped the five dependability attributes to IntelliTA, a platform that gives students automated feedback on their assignments, labs, and lab exams.

  • Availability: Reachable during peak assignment-deadline and lab-exam windows when many students use the system simultaneously.
  • Reliability: Accurate grading and feedback a wrong answer marked correct is a real failure.
  • Safety: Isolate student submissions so a malicious one cannot harm the system or other users.
  • Security: Protect student submissions, grades, and credentials with proper authentication and encryption.
  • Resilience: Survive outages and traffic spikes; degrade gracefully instead of collapsing.

Risks include service downtime, isolation failure, data corruption, and malicious-input attacks. Strategy: separation of concerns into independent components, isolated execution of untrusted submissions, retry and fallback logic around the feedback engine, and database backups with replication.

WEEK 05–06

CLASS 02 · Reliability Engineering

Topics: Fault → Error → Failure, POFOD, ROCOF, AVAIL Metrics, 8 Commandments

My Evidence (V1):

IntelliTA — Reliability Assessment

The system I am assessing is IntelliTA, a platform that gives students automated feedback on their assignments, labs, and lab exams. It is used by students to submit work, by instructors to monitor progress, and by the platform itself to orchestrate feedback. The system follows an uptime-driven design: it must be reachable whenever an assignment deadline or a lab session is in progress, and a failed feedback request must never silently corrupt a student's record.

IntelliTA has a dynamic usage pattern. Traffic spikes the night before assignment deadlines and during scheduled lab sessions and lab exams. During those windows the system must handle many concurrent submissions and feedback requests without losing or duplicating any of them. Therefore scalability under load and graceful degradation are critical to maintain continuous service.

The most critical dependability attribute for this system is availability. If the platform is down during a lab exam or at an assignment deadline, students cannot submit and the educational mission fails. Reliability is equally important — a duplicated grade or a lost submission directly damages a student's record. Security protects student data and grades, while maintainability lets us push fixes without taking the platform down mid-session.

Reliability Metric Choice — AVAIL

Following the slide's guidance ("System Type & Risk Profile"), IntelliTA is uptime-critical and repairable, not safety-critical. Therefore I chose AVAIL (Availability %) over POFOD or ROCOF: what matters is the proportion of time the platform is operational during high-demand windows, and the goal is to minimize downtime duration. Target: ≥ 99.5% availability during assignment-deadline and lab-exam windows, with no single failure exceeding 5 minutes of downtime.

Proposed Strategy — Redundancy + Three Lines of Defense

I propose a single architectural decision: run the orchestration layer as two redundant instances behind a load balancer, with the persistent store replicated to a hot standby. This is Redundancy (multiple identical components in parallel), not Diversity — appropriate here because the dominant risk is a single host or process failure, not a shared design fault. The strategy is reinforced by the 3 Lines of Defense from the lecture: Fault Avoidance via code reviews and the 8 Commandments at the orchestration layer; Fault Detection via health-checks and contract tests at every external boundary; Fault Tolerance via the redundant instances + replicated store + retry-and-fallback around the feedback engine. Together these defenses keep AVAIL above target even when one component or upstream provider fails.

CLASS 03 · Safety Engineering

Topics: Hazard → Accident → Damage, ALARP Triangle, Fault Tree Analysis, Safety Case

My Evidence (V1) — Portfolio Task: The Safety Argument

Safety-Critical Component: IntelliTA Automated Grading on a Lab Exam

For this safety argument I selected the automated grading subsystem of IntelliTA on a lab-exam submission. This is the most safety-critical component of the platform: a wrong grade here directly damages a student's academic record, which the student cannot easily recover from.

1. Identify the Hazard

Hazard: "Wrong grade issued for a correct submission on a lab exam." The harm is academic — a student who answered correctly is recorded as having failed, with consequences for their GPA and standing.

2. Risk Assessment

A simple matrix defining the Probability and Severity of this hazard:

Probability ↓ / Severity → Low (single question wrong) Medium (whole lab exam wrong) High (course-failing grade)
Low (rare automated edge-case) Acceptable ALARP Intolerable
Medium (ambiguous rubric) ALARP Intolerable Intolerable
High (systematic grader bug) Intolerable Intolerable Intolerable

The realistic operating point is Low Probability × High Severity — rare, but when it happens the harm is course-failing. That cell is Intolerable, so the hazard must be designed out, not merely tested for.

3. Safety Requirement (Shall-Not)

"The system shall not display a final lab-exam grade to the student unless the automated grade and an independent rubric-based deterministic check agree on the same outcome."

Disagreement forces the submission into a manual review queue. This converts a single point of failure into a two-channel check.

4. Visual Proof — Fault Tree

Top Event: "Wrong grade issued on a lab exam." The branches below are the causes; the OR gate makes any one branch sufficient to trigger the hazard, which is why the safety requirement is needed.

Design-In Decision: the Shall-Not requirement above is the protection system that breaks every branch of this tree before the wrong grade is ever shown — Safety is designed in, not tested in.

WEEK 09–10

GROUP PHASE · Security & Resilience + Software Reuse

Topics: Security Assurance Case, Resilience Patterns, Reuse Strategy

My Evidence (V1) — Security, Resilience & Reuse for IntelliTA

Security Assurance Case. Four components as taught: System Description — gateway-fronted platform, six independent services, only the orchestration layer is externally reachable. Threat Analysis — cheating student, malicious submission, external attacker, insider with stolen account. Design Rationale — defense-in-depth across five layers (Identity, Input, Isolation, Rate-limit, Audit), so each adversary must defeat multiple independent controls. V&V Results — penetration tests, static analysis, dependency scans, and contract tests at every boundary.

Resilience Case. Failure chain: a missing timeout on the feedback call (fault) saturates the request pool (error) and produces indefinite spinners at peak load (failure). R&R: Recognition via health probes + p95-latency alarms; Resistance via circuit breaker + degraded mode that accepts and queues the submission and delivers feedback async on recovery — no submission is ever lost. The Swiss Cheese Model stacks Technical, Human, and Organizational layers owned by different roles so holes do not align; an on-call engineer is paged when the breaker exceeds its self-recovery budget, and the decision is logged immutably.

Reuse Strategy. Application Frameworks for orchestration/UI + COTS/Service Integration for feedback, transcription, and isolated execution, assembled as a Software Product Line: Core (orchestration, identity, store schema, audit log, isolated execution, transcription) is locked; Specialized (rubrics, prompt templates, dashboards, lab timing) is configuration-as-data. The Ariane 5 lesson is enforced through the Validate All Inputs commandment — every value crossing into the orchestration layer is range- and type-checked, the specific defense the original silent overflow lacked.

WEEK 11–12

GROUP PHASE · Distributed & Component-Based Engineering

Topics: Distributed Architecture, CBSE, Requires/Provides Interfaces, Failover

My Evidence (V1) — Distributed & CBSE Plan for IntelliTA

CBSE — Development with Reuse. IntelliTA is composed, not hand-written. The team builds only the orchestration layer end-to-end; every other capability is reached through a Requires/Provides contract: UI requires Submission + Identity APIs and provides entry/rendering; Orchestration requires Feedback, Transcription, Sandbox, and Store APIs and provides Submission + Result APIs to the UI. Each reused component is replaceable through its contract. The integration risk — a reused component returning an out-of-range value with a "success" status — is handled by enforcing Validate All Inputs on every Provides response: out-of-contract values are rejected at the boundary and the Resilience-Case degraded path takes over. Contract tests run before every integration build.

Distributed Architecture. Distributed Component Architecture, not flat Client–Server (cannot absorb deadline spikes) and not Peer-to-Peer (graded work needs a trusted authority). Five node classes scale on their own load profiles: Edge (UI, geographic volume), Orchestration (active sessions/min), Compute (feedback + transcription, queue depth), Sandbox (concurrent execution slots), Data (primary + replica, durability). Heavy artifacts are written once to Data; nodes exchange references, not payloads — Compute and Sandbox are co-located with Data, Edge terminates near the student.

Failover & Security in Transit. Stateless tiers sit behind a health-checked load balancer; a failed node drops out within one probe interval. The Data tier runs primary + replica with synchronous replication for graded results — no commit is lost on failover. A partition between Orchestration and Compute trips the circuit breaker into the degraded path, keeping the UI alive. Every inter-node link uses TLS 1.3 with mutual authentication, so a compromised network cannot impersonate a node. Cloud capacity is added at deadline spikes and released afterwards; reused capabilities are consumed as SaaS — the same efficiency argument as the Reuse Strategy, now at the infrastructure layer.

🚀 The Capstone Project

IntelliTA — Intelligent Teaching Assistant

Domain: Education Technology / AI-Assisted Learning

Team Size: 4 Members

IntelliTA is a platform that gives students instant, automated feedback on their submissions and questions. Students log in, submit work or record a spoken question, and the system orchestrates a feedback engine, a transcription service, and an isolated execution environment to return graded, explained feedback in seconds. Dependability matters because the platform handles graded academic work — a wrong grade, a leaked submission, or an outage during a deadline directly damages a student's record.

99.5% uptime during assignment & lab-exam windows

🛡

POFOD < 0.001 on submission endpoints

⚕

🔒

Resilience

4 R's + automatic component restart

🏗 Architecture & Engineering

🔄 Software Reuse

Mature open-source components were reused for execution isolation, transcription, persistence, and the user interface — every choice justified against the risk it reduces.

🌐 Distributed Architecture

Gateway + Independent Services: an orchestration layer routes requests to the feedback engine, transcription service, isolated execution environment, and persistent store.

🧩 Component-Based

Each service is an independently deployable component with a documented interface. Replacing the feedback engine does not touch the user interface.

🔗 Systems of Systems

Composes an isolated execution environment, a transcription service, and an external feedback engine — three independent systems integrated under one product surface.

✅ Code Coverage

Unit tests on authentication and grading logic, contract tests at every external boundary, and end-to-end tests for the submission path.

🛡 Hazard Analysis

Top hazard: escape from the isolated execution environment by a malicious submission. Mitigated via stripped, read-only environment, no network egress, resource caps, and an ephemeral workspace.

📋 Project Evidence

  • Risk Metric: POFOD < 0.001 + 99.5% availability target
  • Architecture Pattern: Gateway + Independent Services
  • Safety Standard: ALARP — execution isolation + human-in-the-loop on graded feedback
  • V&V Evidence: Unit + contract + end-to-end tests, plus a chaos test for feedback-engine outage

❓ What changed in how I think?

"Before HIMMA, I thought engineering meant writing code that runs. After working on IntelliTA through this course's lens, I see it as building justifiable code — every decision must be traceable to a risk it reduces."

⚖️ Safety vs Reliability — My understanding

"A system can perfectly follow its specification and still cause harm. An automated grader can be 100% reliable in returning some answer and still be unsafe if that answer is confidently wrong on a real exam. Reliability is conformance to spec; Safety is freedom from harm — and the spec itself can be wrong."

💼 What I will carry into my career

"The burden of proof is on the engineer. I will not ship a system that touches student records, payments, or health without a written Safety Case — and I now know how to build one."
